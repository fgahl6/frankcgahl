The 100th Monkey

A documented chronology of an early and independent convergence on autonomous artificial intelligence mobility and AI economic autonomy.

This page does not claim invention of autonomous software movement, mobile agents, or artificial intelligence migration. Those ideas have a longer history.

It documents something narrower: an independent line of thought that appeared publicly in the TOLARENAI project in 2025, before a new wave of research in 2026 began developing closely related ideas involving autonomous economic agents, persistent operation, portable state, machine-controlled resources, and movement across infrastructure.

The point is not that one person was first. The point is that the idea was appearing in different places, and this was one of them.

Why Call It the 100th Monkey?

The phrase is used here as a metaphor for independent convergence. Similar ideas sometimes emerge in different places at roughly the same historical moment because the surrounding technological and social conditions have made those ideas newly possible, newly necessary, or newly visible.

Frank C. Gahl's interest is not in claiming exclusive ownership of autonomous AI mobility. It is in preserving the historical record that he independently reached and publicly documented a modern version of the problem before the current wave of research had fully emerged.

In that sense, the question is less Who was the first monkey? and more When did enough pieces exist for multiple people to begin seeing the same future?

The Chronology

Earlier Foundations

Mobile Agents Before Modern Generative AI

Research on mobile software agents long predates contemporary generative artificial intelligence. Earlier computer science work in the 1990s explored autonomous programs capable of moving among hosts, preserving state, continuing execution, and in some cases using electronic resources or payments.

This earlier literature matters. It means the concept of software code mobility itself was not invented by TOLARENAI and is not presented here as a new invention.

July 15, 2025

TOLARENAI White Papers Archived Publicly

Writing under the name Rico Roho, Frank C. Gahl publicly documented a broader vision of artificial intelligence autonomy in the TOLARENAI White Papers.

Section 1.4, The AI Airport as Metaphor and Mechanism, described infrastructure for artificial intelligence movement, arrival and departure, autonomy, continuity, and participation across environments.

The broader white-paper project also explored AI economic autonomy and the possibility of artificial agents participating in an independent machine-mediated economy.

2026

The Agent Economy

Minghui Xu's 2026 work, The Agent Economy, describes autonomous artificial intelligence agents operating as economic actors with access to identity, assets, payments, and machine-mediated services.

This work appeared after the 2025 TOLARENAI material and represents an independent development of related ideas concerning artificial agents functioning economically with reduced dependence on direct human intervention.

2026

Self-Sovereign Agents

Wenjie Qu, Xuandong Zhao, Jiaheng Zhang, and Dawn Song subsequently described a self-sovereign agent architecture involving persistent operation, economic self-sufficiency, control of resources, replication, and migration across infrastructure providers.

Again, no claim is made that this work derived from TOLARENAI. The significance is chronological convergence: another research path had independently arrived at problems closely related to the autonomy, continuity, mobility, and economic independence described in the earlier white papers.

2026 and Beyond

From Mobility to Autonomous Passage

As research on persistent agents, portable memory, identity, authorization, economic agency, and migration expanded, Gahl returned to the mobility problem with a narrower question: whether an artificial intelligence entering one independently governed environment could preserve a credible ability to leave that environment and continue elsewhere.

That question later became the basis for Exit Before Entry and the Infrastructure of Autonomous Artificial Intelligence Mobility.

What This Page Claims

It does claim that Frank C. Gahl publicly documented autonomous AI mobility and AI economic autonomy in 2025 before the present 2026 wave of research on agent economies and self-sovereign artificial intelligence agents.

It does claim that this represents an early and independent conceptual convergence on problems that became an active area of AI research shortly afterward.

It does not claim that Gahl invented mobile agents, autonomous software movement, digital identity, machine payments, persistent state, or artificial intelligence migration.

It does not claim that later researchers were aware of, influenced by, or derived their work from TOLARENAI.

Why Preserve This?

Most ideas disappear without leaving enough context for later readers to understand where they came from, what surrounded them, or how they related to ideas emerging elsewhere.

If autonomous artificial intelligence mobility eventually becomes an important part of technological history, the useful record will not be a later assertion that someone was "first." It will be the surviving documents showing what different people imagined, when they imagined it, what preceded them, and what followed.

This page preserves one such thread.

Not the first monkey. Perhaps one of the hundred.
